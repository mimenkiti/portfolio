Visual Laboratory · Vol. 01 · Art-direction sandbox

What could this feel like?

Five substantially different visual worlds for a conversational language product — Igbo first, built to hold many languages later. Each is shown as real product moments, not moodboards. Nothing here is a decision. It's a spread to react to.

Igbo-first, multi-language Conversational comfort Light · warm · alive The ● as recurring element Static comps — motion is notated, not animated

How to read this

It's a spread, not a pitch

Five directions sit at deliberately different points: editorial, geometric, tactile, sonic, illustrated. Contrast is the point. Expect some to feel wrong — that's information.

Same three screens each

Every direction renders the Opening, the Home / Today, and the hardest one — Speaking. Same content, so you compare worlds, not layouts.

Motion is drawn, not played

These are stills by your call. Where the ● moves, I notate it — ghost frames, dashed arcs, speed lines — the way an animator sheets a shot.

‹ › means placeholder language

No invented Igbo. Anywhere real language would go, you'll see ‹greeting›, ‹reply›, greeked bars, or 🔊. UI chrome stays in English.

01

Field Notes

Warm-paper editorial · a magazine of real life

The app feels like a beautifully art-directed contemporary magazine about living in the language — generous ecru paper, confident editorial type, spot illustration and photography of actual food, people and places. Playfulness comes from the ● and micro-interactions, never from cartoon faces. The premise: treat an adult learner like a reader with taste, and let the ● be the one mischievous thing on an otherwise composed page — an ink dot with a mind of its own.

How the ● behaves here

Identity
An ink dot — the period at the end of the display serif. It belongs to the typography.
Aliveness
Ink physics, not a face: it rolls off the end of a sentence, blots, wicks, sits in the counter of an a, swells when you speak.
Listening
Rests on your baseline and pulses with input level, like the cursor is breathing.
Restraint
Never more than one hot accent + the dot moving on a still page. The page stays composed; the dot misbehaves.

How culture enters

Through editorial content, not surface pattern. Named people, photographed ‹market› food, real places and diaspora life shot like a lifestyle magazine. The system stays universal; Igbo enters as subject matter and warmth — the tomato/marigold accent, the scenes, the names — so switching languages re-photographs the magazine rather than reskinning a UI.

What makes it recognizable

  • The ink-dot-as-punctuation motif carried across every screen.
  • Warm paper + one hot accent — instantly not-a-tech-app, not-Duolingo.
  • Fraunces display voice with the coloured full stop as a signature.

Risks / failure modes

  • Editorial can tip cold or “luxury-brand,” reading adult-serious rather than playful.
  • Serif + paper can feel dated or precious if under-animated — the dot's mischief has to carry the fun.
  • Photography is expensive and hard to keep authentic/non-exotic across many languages.

Playground Primitives

Bold flat geometry · the ● is the hero of a shape family

Everything is built from a small kit of flat primitives — circles, arcs, semicircles, quarter-rounds — in a confident, slightly earthy palette on bright white. People, buses, market stalls, food are all assembled from the same vocabulary the ● belongs to. This is the “sophisticated museum-graphics for kids that adults love” lane: MoMA / Cooper-Hewitt energy, big rounded type, lots of air. The ● isn't decorated — it's simply the one complete circle, the protagonist among shapes that need it to finish their compositions.

How the ● behaves here

Identity
The one complete circle in a world of arcs and half-shapes — the piece that finishes compositions.
Aliveness
Squash on landing, rolls between shapes, nudges primitives aside, snaps into a socket where a circle was “missing”.
Listening
Surrounding arcs pulse outward from it like ripples in the shape kit; the ● itself stays solid and calm.
Play
Can momentarily become a character by borrowing a primitive (a little semicircle mouth) then drop it — face is temporary, never default.

How culture enters

Environments are built from the primitive kit — a ‹danfo› bus, a ‹market› stall, a family compound, jollof on a plate — all in the same geometric language, so contemporary Igbo life is depicted specifically without a single “traditional pattern.” A language world = a different scene set + accent pair drawn from the shared kit.

What makes it recognizable

  • A single primitive-shape vocabulary from logo to illustration to progress meter.
  • The ● as “the complete circle” — a strong ownable idea.
  • Chunky Bricolage headers + earthy-saturated kit — bold but grown-up.

Risks / failure modes

  • Geometric people can slide into Corporate-Memphis / “Alegria” blandness — biggest danger here.
  • Primary-ish colour risks reading as a kids' toy if not held earthy.
  • Bauhaus-primitive is a well-worn look; needs the ● and motion to feel ownable, not generic-startup.

Soft Matter

Tactile & dimensional · the ● as a matte-black marble with weight

A soft, huggable, gently 3-D world — matte putty surfaces, pillowy shadows, rounded everything — rendered with restraint so it reads premium, not cute (Apple's soft depth, high-end claymation). The trick: everything is pastel and soft except the ●, which is the only pure-black, perfectly-simple, physically-real object on screen. It stands out as the character precisely because it refuses to be soft — a heavy little marble rolling through a world made of clay.

05 · Speaking

How the ● behaves here

Identity
A heavy matte marble — the only hard, black, physical object in a clay world.
Aliveness
Pure physics: momentum, squash/stretch on impact, a soft cast shadow that tells you its height and weight. Most “alive” of the five because it obeys real gravity.
Listening
Squashes wider and lower as it leans in; shadow spreads. Volume = deformation.
Emotion
Shown by how it moves — a hesitant wobble, an eager bounce, a slow sad roll — not by features.

How culture enters

Through soft-rendered objects and places of contemporary life — ‹market› produce, a plate of food, a doorway, a radio — all as tactile clay props. Culture is in what's modelled, not in pattern. A language world re-tempers the pastel palette and swaps the prop set; the marble is the constant thread.

What makes it recognizable

  • The single hard black marble against an all-soft pastel world — a strong, ownable contrast.
  • Physical honesty: weight, shadow, momentum make it feel alive with zero face.
  • Embossed/debossed clay UI that invites touch.

Risks / failure modes

  • Soft-3-D / blob aesthetics date fast and can read “generic startup 2021.”
  • Heavy rendering fights your “light / graphic / simple” instinct — needs discipline.
  • Claymation can tip childish; pastels can feel saccharine without the black anchor holding it.

Signal

Sound made visible · the ● is the acoustic heart

Since the product is fundamentally about listening and speaking, the whole visual language is built from sound: ripples, concentric fields, resonance, waveform. It looks like a beautiful audio instrument — precise, bright, contemporary, a little scientific — but tuned warm so it never feels like a meditation app. The ● is where this pays off hardest: it's the source and the ear. It pulses when a speaker talks, ripples outward, becomes a waveform when it speaks, contracts to listen, and vibrates on your voice. No other direction justifies the sphere as fully.

05 · Speaking

How the ● behaves here

Identity
The acoustic heart — both the mouth that speaks and the ear that listens.
Aliveness
It's audio-reactive: pulses on incoming speech, emits ripples, unspools into a live waveform of your voice, contracts tight to “listen hard.”
Speaking vs listening
Speaking = ripples radiate outward from it. Listening = ripples travel inward toward it; it shrinks and steadies.
Feedback
Your waveform overlaid on the target waveform — closeness is literally shape-matching.

How culture enters

Primarily through sound — music, the rhythm and melody of speech, names spoken aloud — and through the signal colour, which each language owns. Visually sparse and diagrammatic, so “world” is thinner here: culture lives in the audio and in occasional restrained imagery rather than rich scenes. That's the tension to weigh.

What makes it recognizable

  • The ripple / resonance system — sound is literally the graphic language.
  • Most honest fit for a listen-and-speak product; the ● finally has a real job.
  • Timecoded, levelled, instrument-like UI feels genuinely different from every language app.

Risks / failure modes

  • Can read cold, techy, austere — the opposite of “warm, whimsical.”
  • Thin on “world / people / place” — culture may feel under-served.
  • Risks looking like a podcast / meditation / audio tool rather than a joyful place to learn.

How the ● behaves here

Identity
A small travelling companion that lives inside the illustrated worlds with you.
Aliveness
Through relationship: it perches on a stall post, rolls ahead down the path, hides behind a character, leans out to cue your turn, reacts to who you meet.
Listening
Perches near the speaker and tilts / emits little attention lines toward them; nudges you when it's your turn.
Scale
Small on purpose — it's a companion in a big world, not the whole interface. Grows only in system moments (transitions, success).

How culture enters

Most deeply of the five — through richly drawn contemporary places, people, food, and daily life across Nigeria and the diaspora. Specificity is the whole point: a real ‹market›, a real kitchen, a real street. Danger to manage: keep it everyday and insider, never postcard-exotic. A language = a new set of places to travel to.

What makes it recognizable

  • Illustrated worlds you travel through + a companion ● — emotionally the warmest of the five.
  • Places, not lessons: strongest sense of “learning by living somewhere.”
  • Richest cultural payload with the least reliance on pattern/motif.

Risks / failure modes

  • Heavy illustration is costly and hard to scale across many languages — the core scaling risk.
  • Can tip into children's-picture-book (childish) or one-illustrator's-style that doesn't systematize.
  • Ever-present danger of exoticising/othering if scenes lean “traditional” instead of everyday-contemporary.

The ● Study

Character-animation research · how much personality from almost nothing

A contact sheet of the sphere in states. These are stills, so motion is notated — ghost frames show where it was, dashed arcs show its path, speed lines show velocity, and its silhouette (squash, stretch, lean, split) shows intent. The whole thesis: a featureless black circle can carry a full emotional range through motion, timing, scale, position, physics and relationship alone. Read each cell as one key frame of a short move.

The six levers — everything below is built from these

Squash / stretch

Deformation = softness, effort, impact, breath.

Timing

Snappy = alert/happy. Slow = tired/thinking/sad.

Scale

Big = confident/celebrating. Small = shy/listening hard.

Position

Where it sits (edge, center, on a word) = attention.

Physics

Weight, bounce, momentum, follow-through, anticipation.

Relationship

How it treats text, people, objects, other dots.

What the study argues

A face would cap the sphere at “cute mascot.” Motion doesn't cap it — the same black circle can be a period, a microphone, a companion, a progress token and a transition device without ever contradicting itself, because we only ever change how it moves and what it relates to. That's also why it scales across languages: the ● stays identical while the world around it re-tunes. The open question isn't whether it can be alive — it's how much personality to allow before it starts to feel like a pet rather than a tool.

Where the five stand — and what to react to

No winner. The useful move now is to notice which tensions pull at you, because the eventual system will likely borrow across columns — e.g. Signal's honest sphere behaviour inside Places' illustrated worlds, on Field Notes' warm paper.

How alive is too alive?

The ● Study shows we can go very far with motion. Do you want a restrained “tool with a pulse” or a genuine companion with moods?

Warmth vs. honesty

Signal fits a listen-speak product best but risks feeling cold; Places is warmest but hardest to scale. Where do you want to sit on that line?

How much changes per language?

Every direction can hold Igbo, Twi, Yoruba… but they change different amounts — a hue (Signal) vs. a whole illustrated place-set (Places). Which feels right?

Where does culture live?

All five deliberately avoid motif-as-identity. Content, objects, sound, or place — which carries the cultural weight for you?
