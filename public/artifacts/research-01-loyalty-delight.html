Research 01 · Loyalty & delight · for the ● language product

What kind of product would someone enjoy learning with every day

A grounded study of what creates delight, habit, and 190-day loyalty — in Duolingo, in other learning apps, and in great consumer products — turned into a critique of our five directions and a set of design principles. Not a moodboard. Not a new direction.

The one lesson, if you read nothing else

Durable delight lives inside the core act, not around it. Every product people still love on Day 190 — Duolingo's satisfying lesson loop, Drops' drag-to-match, CapWords' capture, ELSA's red→green, Things' checkbox — puts its joy in the mechanic itself. The surrounding scaffolding (streaks, XP, leagues, confetti) creates Day-1 delight and Day-190 fatigue, guilt, or anxiety. For us, the core act is speaking and being understood. That is where our delight budget must go — which is exactly why the ● and speech-as-material matter more than any wrapper we put around them.

Parts 1 & 2 · The reference landscape, through one lens

What each product actually gets right

Researched against current (2024–2026) versions, reviews, design commentary and real user sentiment. Each distilled to: what it nails, how it holds up over time, the mechanism worth stealing, and where it curdles.

A · Learning products

Duolingo— the delight engine, studied honestly

Why it delights: it manufactures constant, legible, multi-sensory wins on a variable schedule, wrapped in a lovable, flawed character who gives your effort a face — with near-zero friction and softened failure. Lessons are 3–5 min, one prompt on screen, engineered to end on an easy item so you close on a win; the progress bar advances even on wrong answers; wrong answers show the right one instantly with a neutral "GOT IT," not shame.

Steal: character-as-interface (reminders feel relational, not corporate); end every session on a win; progress that never regresses; non-punitive failure copy; one signature multi-sensory success moment; a grace mechanic (streak freeze) so one miss ≠ abandonment.

What curdles by Day 190: the metric (app-opens) drifts from the goal (fluency) — the deepest, most-repeated complaint is "I finished the whole tree and still can't hold a conversation." Streaks weaponise loss aversion into midnight-obligation anxiety; hearts/ads/refills monetise friction Duolingo itself created; leagues breed hollow speed-tapping; the tone can read juvenile to adults.

Speak— speech as the whole interface

Best-in-class safety engineering for a vulnerable act: correct words light up, errors stay neutral, corrections appear inside your own sentence as a fill-in, and you can code-switch (drop English words mid-sentence) to keep talking. A plain-language "why?" tutor sits in the flow.

Steal: light-up-the-right-words; failure-as-completion not rejection; accept code-switching (huge for heritage speakers with partial vocab).

Trap: leniency taken too far — perfect scores for reversed word order breed false mastery; feedback is thin and non-cumulative; the AI ends every turn with a question, killing conversational rhythm.

Memrise— real humans in real rooms

"Learn with locals" clips — actual people on streets and in kitchens saying phrases with slang, speed, emotion, humour. The strongest ear-and-courage builder of any app here, and culture delivered through voice, not decoration.

Steal: real, multiple, revisitable native speakers as the primary cultural signal — culture enters through the ear.

Trap: the speak-back layer (MemBot) is a shallow chatbot exercise; content thins fast; killing community courses burned trust.

Babbel & Busuu— adult, calm, "learning-led not dopamine-led"

Babbel is the boring-educational-software trap done tastefully — trustworthy, scaffolded, no streak anxiety, but speaking is nearly absent. Busuu's magic is human correction: submit a phrase, a real native speaker corrects it, and you reciprocate — "a person chose to help you" is a powerful belonging + safety signal.

Steal: a calm adult tone with no anxiety loops; async human warmth & reciprocity as a belonging signal.

Trap: both stall at a "B1 plateau" of recycled scenarios; both barely let you actually speak.

Drops— the learning act is the toy

No typing, ever — you drag words onto illustrations, swipe to sort, tap syllables. The quiz is a physics-of-dragging interaction, so delight lives inside the mechanic. A hard 5-minute session is the spine ("a gaming, not gamified, app"). Radically low density: one word, one image, one gesture.

Steal: make the learning act a gesture, not an answer; radical low density; soft failure (word slides back, no penalty).

Trap: teaches words, not language — breadth without the ability to form a sentence.

CapWords— vocabulary anchored to your own world (ADA 2025)

Point your camera at a real object → it's recognised, peeled into a sticker, and the word attaches. The magic is capture + ownership: your rooms become a personal, autobiographical dictionary. Delight is fully inside the core act; no points needed.

Steal: anchor language to the learner's real life; progress as a growing personal artifact, not a leaderboard.

Trap: thin on grammar/conversation; a collection can sprawl without structure.

ELSA Speak + Brilliant— feedback-as-transformation; touch-then-explain

ELSA renders your pronunciation as phonemes lighting green / yellow / red in real time — the correction is the reward, granular and motivating (though gameable, and it scores sounds not conversation). Brilliant lets you manipulate a concept before any notation, and getting it wrong opens an explorable path — the gold standard for "playful yet grown-up."

Steal: make feedback a visible, warm transformation; let people touch the thing before you explain it; failure as a doorway, not a dead-end.

B · Consumer products (where people just like being in the app)

Finch— attachment engineered without needing a face

You design the bird before you disclose anything (ownership with zero friction). Then your real self-care fuels its energy and 8-hour adventures; it returns with a story your reply shapes, so its personality accretes from your choices — co-authored. Users: "I feel so responsible for this dang bird… it helped me get out of bed." The bird is a living progress bar you love because it reflects your own effort back at you.

Steal (all faceless): invest-before-disclose; reflected effort; reactivity/contingency; ritual rhythm; accreted memory; reciprocity + shame-free failure.

Trap: reflected-effort bonds can tip into guilt/obligation; density & tap-fatigue; "too cute" for some adults.

Focus Friend (Bean)— attachment from behaviour alone

Bean knits while you focus — effort visualised in real time — and visibly saddens if you quit early. That's the whole emotional engine: reactivity + a persistent world you curate (and don't want to abandon). Cozy, non-punitive, minimal. Went from zero to Google Play App of the Year.

Steal: the companion reacts to effort in real time; one gentle emotional lever, not constant performance; loss aversion via a world you built, not a streak you fear.

Headspace— mature whimsy; emotion without literal faces

Round, edgeless characters personify your inner states through metaphor (steam for anger, not a scowl); colour acts as a "dimmer switch." Serious practice made approachable through restraint and metaphor, never childishness. Warmth is a world/brand relationship, not a pet.

Steal: abstract shapes + warm motion + metaphor carry real emotion with no face; "space, air to breathe."

Things— the non-cute benchmark; affection from competence

Zero characters, deep affection — because it treats you with respect and reliability. The Magic Plus button, the satisfying check + haptic tick, items sliding away: delight is choreography, not decoration. Motion is almost purely information; haptics carry the personality a mascot would. It ages into furniture precisely because it never spent a novelty budget.

Steal: haptics + one signature micro-interaction as the emotional payload; motion-as-information; whitespace & typography as warmth; restraint as the thing that survives to Day 190.

Airbnb— warmth + trust at mainstream scale

Complex info (price math, reviews, policies) made approachable through hierarchy, an 8px grid, and a bespoke warm typeface (Cereal) doing "90% of the brand feel" — because 90% of UI is text. One consistent design system means every new surface feels like the same trustworthy place. Fun is kept out of the transactional core on purpose.

Steal: typography as voice; one system so every screen feels like home; put warmth around the edges, keep the core act calm.

Award winners & a wordless game— delight without cuteness or budget

ADA'25CapWords (delight fused into learning) · ADA'24Bears Gratitude (character-led ritual), Rooms (tactile 3D detail), Rytmos (one gesture → complexity), NYT Games (typographic restraint) · ADA'25PBJ (personality via rhythm/haptics, no mascot) · Play'24Partiful (expressive-but-usable). And Sky: Children of the Light — a faceless companion earns love through ritual, progressive revelation and reciprocity, no dialogue, all gesture and light. Validation that our ● can be a presence, not a pet.

Part 3 · Loyalty & delight

Why someone actually opens a product every day

Six different forces get confused under "engagement." The strongest products stack several; the weak ones fake the ones they lack. Pulling them apart tells us which to lean on.

Functional "I need this."

The real-world reason. Here is our quiet superpower: Duolingo's functional value is weak — nobody needs it — which is exactly why it leans so hard on manufactured streaks. A heritage language has intrinsically strong functional value: talk to your grandmother, understand your family, belong. We should build on that, not paper over its absence with gamification.

Habit "It's part of my routine."

Built from a low-friction daily unit + ritual rhythm + a grace mechanic. The trigger matters morally: Duolingo triggers with anxiety (you'll lose your streak); Finch/Bean trigger with a small return you look forward to. Same loop, opposite feeling on Day 190.

Affection "I like this product."

The ● companion (reflected effort, reactivity, shame-free failure), the warmth of the voice, tactile micro-interactions, a signature sound. Note Things: affection with zero characters, purely from competence + haptics. Affection does not require cuteness.

Progress "I can see myself getting somewhere."

Must measure the real thing. "You can now meet someone" beats "+50 XP" because it's true and it matters. Best delivered as a growing personal artifact (CapWords) — a record of conversations you can now actually have.

Delight "It occasionally makes me smile."

Surprise, humour, the feedback-that-transforms, a rare ● behaviour. Powerful precisely because it's occasional and variable — the honest version of Duolingo's variable reward, tied to joy instead of loss.

Identity "This is for people like me."

The most under-used and, for us, the most powerful. A heritage-language product can make someone feel seen in a way a global app never will — contemporary and diaspora life, real voices, names, food, humour. This is where we can decisively out-resonate Duolingo.

How the strongest products combine them

Weak products lead with habit + delight and hope you don't notice the functional value is thin (Duolingo's structural bet). Strong, durable products lead with functional + progress + identity and use habit and delight as honest servants. Our natural stack is unusual and favourable: lead with identity + functional (heritage belonging), make progress honest and capability-based, earn affection through the ●, and spend habit + delight scaffolding lightly — because we don't need to manufacture a reason to return. The reason is real.

Gamification — genuinely useful vs. manipulative

Useful when the mechanic celebrates something the user actually values: legible progress toward real capability, a gentle habit cue, a grace mechanic that forgives a missed day, a milestone that marks a true achievement. Manipulative when it detaches from the goal (rewarding app-opens, not learning), weaponises loss aversion (streak dread), monetises manufactured friction (hearts sold back to you), or breeds hollow competition (leagues). The test: does this mechanic celebrate something the user values, or manufacture an anxiety and sell relief? We keep the first kind and refuse the second.

Part 4 · Our criteria, pressure-tested

Do our stated qualities survive contact with the research?

Mostly yes — with sharper edges, and two additions the research forces.

Delightful — holds, but redirect it: put delight in the core (speaking, being understood), not in decoration that fatigues.
Friendly — the central UX problem, and the research names the exact solution and its trap: warm framing (light-up-right, failure-as-fill-in, shame-free like Finch/Bean) plus honest feedback. Speak proves warmth alone tips into false mastery; nobody has nailed warm framing with truthful, pattern-level feedback — that gap is ours to take.
Habitual — holds, but trigger on intrinsic value, not anxiety. Add a grace mechanic; refuse streak dread.
Clean — strongly supported (Drops, Things, Headspace: one thing on screen). The learning is the cognitive load; the UI must not compete.
Mature-playful — supported and achievable without cuteness (Things, Brilliant, PBJ, Headspace): playfulness via motion, haptics, typography and restraint.
Grassroots / lived-in — this is the Identity lever, your biggest advantage. Delivered through real voices and everyday/diaspora life (Memrise's locals), never museum or motif.
Dynamic — this is Signal/speech-as-material, and it's genuinely ownable and on-message (ELSA, Speak).
Ownable — the signature test (Things = whitespace + type + micro-interaction; Duolingo = green + Duo). Our candidates: the ●'s motion signature, speech-as-visual, a warm type voice, a signature sound.
Culturally alive — same as grassroots; carried by people/voices/food/places/contemporary life.
Scalable — the ● stays constant; the world re-tunes through voices/content/accent, not bespoke art (this is what indicts Places and rescues everything else). One DLS-style system.
Long-lasting — the Day-190 filter. Restraint ages (Things); novelty and cuteness curdle. Capability-progress and reflected-effort attachment age well; streak-anxiety and confetti don't.

Two criteria to add

Honest. Progress and feedback must reflect real conversational capability, never a vanity metric — because "the number rewards opening the app, not learning" is the single failure that eventually loses even loyal users. Sound-first. For a listen/speak product, sound (voices, a signature success chime, haptics) is a first-class material and one of the most scalable cultural carriers — it deserves to be a criterion, not an afterthought.

The pattern is clear: Signal is the strongest structural spine (delight-in-core, clean, scalable, ownable, ages well) but it is the coldest and thinnest on culture. Places is the warmest and most culturally alive but the least scalable and least clean. The work ahead is not to pick one — it's to give Signal the warmth and cultural life of Places without importing its costs.

01 · Field Notes — keep the respect, lose the gloss

Keep: the ink-dot-as-punctuation motif (a genuinely ownable idea); warm paper; type-as-voice; the premise of treating an adult like a reader with taste.
Kill: photography as the primary cultural vehicle — it doesn't scale, risks a glossy "magazine about them" gaze, and can feel curated from outside rather than lived.
Modify: pull it from "luxury magazine" toward "living zine"; it's too static day-to-day — it needs Signal's responsiveness to feel alive on repeat.

02 · Playground Primitives — keep the kit, not the people

Keep: the ● as "the one complete circle," and the primitive shape kit as a scalable construction system for objects, icons and environments.
Kill: geometric people — this is the Corporate-Memphis / "Alegria" blandness the research explicitly flags, and it already reads dated.
Modify: use the kit for objects and wayfinding, never for depicting humans; let real voices (not shapes) carry cultural specificity so a "shape market" doesn't become any market.

03 · Soft Matter — keep the marble's body, drop the clay

Keep: the ●'s physics — weight, squash, momentum, a real cast shadow. This is the most physically "alive" reading and maps perfectly onto Finch/Bean's reflected-effort attachment.
Kill: the all-pastel claymorphic world as the overall look — soft-3D dates fast ("generic startup 2021"), tips childish, and fights our light/clean/mature instincts. Its Longevity score is the lowest on the board for a reason.
Modify: extract the weighted-marble ● and set it in a lighter, less trend-bound world; keep the tactility in haptics (Things) rather than heavy rendering.

04 · Signal — keep all of it, warm all of it

Keep: everything structural — speech-as-visual-material, the ● as acoustic heart, feedback-as-transformation, light-up-the-right-words. This is the spine, and it's the one direction that scores H on delight, simplicity, motion, scalability, ownability and longevity.
Kill: the cold "mixing-desk" veneer — timecodes, dB readouts, electric-blue techiness. It reads as an audio tool, not a warm place, and is the sole reason its Warmth and Cultural scores dip.
Modify: treat Signal as behaviour, not skin (see Part 7). Dress it in warmth borrowed from elsewhere: real voices, a warm palette and type voice, and the ● as a companion for affection and habit.

05 · Places — keep the life, make it a "sometimes" layer

Keep: the cultural richness (contemporary + diaspora life, people, food, places), the companion-in-a-world attachment (Finch/Sky), and the "travel into a living language" narrative.
Kill: full bespoke illustration as the everyday UI for every screen — unscalable per language, costly, one-illustrator-style risk, and it competes with the cognitive load of learning (its Simplicity and Scalability are the lowest on the board).
Modify: reserve rich illustration for special moments (arrivals, story scenes, milestones) over a clean everyday UI; let real voices and everyday content carry most of the culture, reducing both the art burden and the exoticising risk.
Part 6 · The ●, deepened

Can you love something with no face? The research says yes

Across Finch, Focus Friend, Headspace and Sky, the face is the least of it. A face accelerates the first read of a specific emotion, but durable affection is built by mechanisms that almost all work faceless — and a black sphere can deliver every one of them through motion, timing, physics, colour temperature, light, sound and behaviour.

The faceless attachment kit (all proven, all available to ●)

  • Reflected effort — the ● is brighter, fuller, steadier because you showed up. Loving it is loving evidence of your own progress (Finch's bird, Bean's knitting). This is the single strongest lever and needs no eyes.
  • Reactivity / contingency — it changes state because of what you did (Bean saddens when you quit). Contingency reads as aliveness.
  • Ritual & rhythm — a consistent return-moment makes it a fixture.
  • Memory / accretion — it remembers, lightly, and shifts over weeks; that makes the character feel co-authored.
  • Invest-before-disclose — let the learner shape it (colour, motion signature, name) before asking anything of them.
  • Reciprocity + shame-free failure — it cares back; a missed day is met with welcome, not guilt (the Finch caution).

Answering your questions directly

How it greets you on return: a quiet wake — it brightens and leans slightly toward you, warmth scaled to how long you were away and how you left last time (gentler and encouraging if you'd been struggling; an eager little rise if you'd nailed it). No "you're back!" fanfare.
Does it remember? Lightly, and in service of learning, not surveillance: the sound or word you struggled with (it returns to it), that you've been consistent (it's fuller), that you named and coloured it.
Does it change over time? Yes, but not as growth-stages-that-pressure you. Its resting motion can mature, its colour deepen, a faint patina of history accrue — calm and earned, never an obligation clock.
Struggle vs. succeed: struggle → it leans in, steadies, softens, waits patiently, encourages — never sighs or looks disappointed (that's the Duo-guilt trap). Succeed → it brightens, hops, and at real moments splits into a little burst and re-merges.
Excited / curious / proud / impatient / bored: excitement = fast upward timing + brightness; curiosity = a lean toward new content; pride = a slow warm swell at a milestone (no confetti); impatience and boredom used very rarely and only playfully, and never aimed at the user's absence — if it's ever "bored," it's quietly amusing itself while you're away, which is charming, not guilt-inducing.
Tease, initiate, celebrate without confetti, respond to your voice, touch text: yes to all — gently, rarely, and mostly as the core interaction itself (it is the mic; it becomes punctuation; it catches a mispronounced syllable). "Help without constantly interrupting" is the governing constraint.

The Day-190 discipline — behaviour frequency

The failure mode you named — "if ● reacts after every tap it becomes annoying" — is solved by budgeting personality across four tiers. Most of the time, the ● is quiet. Attachment comes from consistency + contingency + rare surprise, not constant performance.

Part 7 · Signal & speech as material

Can speech itself be one of our core visual materials?

Yes — and it's our most ownable, on-message and scalable material, because our product is listen → speak → respond → converse. The key reframe from the research: Signal is behaviour, not skin. It's the product's responsiveness layer, and it can live inside any warmer world we choose.

The behavioural vocabulary (separate from any aesthetic)

  • ● reacts to incoming speech — when a speaker talks, it pulses and ripples inward: visibly listening.
  • ● is your microphone — your voice deforms it in real time; the correct parts light up as you go (Speak, ELSA). No giant mic button — the dot is the mic, live.
  • Speech moves through space — a phrase travels from the other voice to you, and your reply travels back: conversation as a rally between two points, not an exercise in a box.
  • Waveform as trace — your utterance leaves a visible shape; getting closer to the target is literal shape-matching (ELSA's transformation, warmed).
  • Rhythm & tone made visible — compression/stretch/ripple for prosody and melody, not just phonemes — which matters for the music of many heritage languages.
  • Text reacts to pronunciation — a word settles when said well; a syllable that needs work glows gently — warm, never a red buzzer.
  • Turn-taking made visual — the "floor" (or the ●) moves between you and the other voice, so a conversation feels like one.
  • Feedback as transformation, not scoring — the red→green warmth, but kept honest: show what to adjust without shame, and resist Speak's over-leniency that teaches nothing.
The discipline

Don't overdesign it. Most speech-visual should be information (Things' motion-as-information — it teaches you by showing you), a little should be reward, and almost none should be decoration. The behavioural logic is the durable asset; the styling (blue, timecodes, dB) is disposable and should be warmed or dropped. Because this layer is built from sound and motion rather than pictures, it is inherently scalable across languages — the single most important structural reason to make speech a core material.

Part 8 · Culture & "grassroots"

"Here is an Igbo course" vs. "Come into this living language"

The difference is present tense. A course preserves a language as heritage (past, curated, singular); a living language is spoken today by real, plural, funny people — including young diaspora. Design communicates the second not through motifs but through voices, people and the everyday.

Where culture actually enters

  • Voices first. Real people, many of them, with real cadence, slang, humour and emotion (Memrise's "learn with locals" is the proof). Multiple generations and diaspora accents. Voice is the richest and most scalable cultural carrier — and it's exactly on-thesis, because culture entering through the ear is the same channel our product already lives in.
  • People as themselves, contemporary. Everyday clothes, real settings, jokes — a cousin texting from Houston, a London auntie — not costumes or patterns. Diaspora life named explicitly.
  • Content as culture. Names, food, places, objects, situations, music, family, slang — woven into the conversations you learn, not applied as decoration.
  • A sense of community on the other side. "A person chose to help you" (Busuu) is a powerful belonging + identity signal; even lightly, the feeling that this language has living speakers you're joining.
  • Humour & expressions. The fastest route to "insider, alive" rather than "institutional." How people actually talk.
The trap to avoid

Treating heritage as heritage — past-tense, preserved, behind glass. Avoid generic "African" visual language, museum/tourism framing, tribal motifs as identity, patterns-as-brand, and nostalgia as the only register. The antidote is present tense, plural voices, everyday life, diaspora inclusion, and humour.

The move that also solves scalability: carry culture through voices + people + content + a colour/sound accent, and keep the visual chrome universal and warm. This resolves the Places dilemma — we get the cultural richness from real voices and everyday content (cheap to produce, deeply specific, hard to exoticise) instead of bespoke illustration per language (expensive, and prone to the postcard gaze). Culture becomes the thing that re-tunes the constant system, exactly as we hoped.

Part 9 · Synthesis

The twelve answers

1 · The most important lessons. Durable delight lives inside the core act, not around it. Attachment does not require a face. Progress must measure the real thing. Restraint ages; novelty and cuteness curdle. And our natural advantages — strong functional value, strong identity resonance — are ones the giants lack.

2 · What Duolingo gets right (don't be afraid to learn it). Character-as-interface makes reminders relational not corporate; every session ends on a win; the progress bar never regresses; failure is instant and non-shaming; success is one signature multi-sensory moment; the daily unit is tiny and frictionless; a grace mechanic forgives a slip.

3 · What not to copy from Duolingo. Optimising a metric (app-opens) that drifts from the goal (fluency); weaponising streaks into anxiety; monetising manufactured friction (hearts/ads); leaderboard-driven hollow competition; and a relentlessly juvenile tone. The famous critique — "finished the tree, still can't converse" — is our reason to exist; we must not import the machine that causes it.

4 · What other learning products teach. Make the learning act a gesture (Drops); anchor language to the learner's real life and let progress be a personal artifact (CapWords); render feedback as a warm, visible transformation (ELSA); let people touch a concept before explaining it (Brilliant); build courage with real human voices (Memrise); keep a calm, adult, anxiety-free tone (Babbel/Busuu); and make speaking safe — light-up-right, failure-as-fill-in, accept code-switching (Speak) — without tipping into false leniency.

5 · What non-learning products teach. Affection can come from pure competence + haptics with zero characters (Things); typography carries voice and one system makes every screen feel like home (Airbnb); a companion earns love through reflected effort, reactivity, ritual and a world you curate — faceless (Finch, Focus Friend, Sky); serious things become approachable through restraint and metaphor, not childishness (Headspace); and delight without cuteness or budget wins awards (PBJ, Rytmos, NYT Games).

6 · Which of our directions stay promising. Signal as the structural spine (delight-in-core, clean, scalable, ownable, ages well). Places' cultural life and companion-in-a-world warmth, demoted to a "sometimes" layer. The ●'s physics from Soft Matter. Field Notes' respect for the adult and the ink-dot motif. Primitives' construction kit for objects.

7 · What to drop. Cold techy Signal styling; the all-pastel claymorphic world; geometric people (Corporate Memphis); full bespoke illustration as everyday UI; and photography as the sole cultural vehicle.

8 · What ● needs to become capable of. Being the microphone and the feedback (core), the punctuation and the companion (character) — quiet by default, expressive through motion/timing/physics/colour/light/sound, remembering lightly, reflecting your effort, forgiving your absence, and budgeting its personality across everyday / occasional / rare / milestone tiers so it stays a presence, not a pet.

9 · How culture should enter. Through the ear and the everyday — real plural voices, contemporary and diaspora life, names, food, humour, community — re-tuning a universal, warm, restrained system. Present tense, never museum.

10 · How delight, habit and progress relate. Delight is the Day-1 hook but can't sustain; habit is the Day-30 scaffolding but is empty without a reason; progress is the Day-190 reason — the felt sense of becoming someone who can converse. They must reinforce, not substitute: delight should celebrate real progress (not distract from its absence), habit should be triggered by intrinsic value (not manufactured anxiety), and progress must be honest. Duolingo's trap is delight + habit substituting for progress. Our advantage is that our progress is real, so we can let it be the star.

11 · Emerging design principles

Put delight in the mouth, not the margins.

The joy must live in speaking and being understood. Streaks, XP and confetti are margins; they fatigue. Spend the design budget on the core act.

The ● is the interface, not a mascot.

Most of the time it's simply the app working — the mic, the feedback, the punctuation. Personality comes from contingency and rare surprise, never constant performance.

Warm framing, honest feedback.

Never a red buzzer on a vulnerable utterance — but never fake leniency either. Show what to adjust, kindly. This gap (warmth + truth) is the one no competitor has filled.

Measure the real thing.

Track capability — "you can meet someone" — not app-opens. Refuse any metric that would let someone feel successful while unable to converse.

Attachment is reflected effort.

The ● is fuller because you showed up; loving it is loving your own progress. Keep failure shame-free so the mirror never guilts.

Culture enters through the ear and the everyday.

Voices, names, food, humour, diaspora life — present tense and plural. The visual chrome stays universal; the world re-tunes through content and sound, not motifs.

Restraint ages; novelty fatigues.

Design for Day 190. Whitespace, type-as-voice, signature micro-interactions and haptics carry warmth without cuteness that curdles.

Spend celebration rarely.

Reserve big moments for real milestones; keep everyday wins quiet and tactile. Scarcity is what keeps the big moments meaningful.

Sound is a first-class material.

A signature success chime, haptics and real voices carry personality and belonging — and they scale across languages by nature.

One system, many worlds.

The ●, the motion grammar and the type/sound signature stay constant; language identity comes from voices, content and accent — so we never redesign per language.

12 · Ingredient combinations to explore next

Stated as principle-blends, not "mash directions together."

A · The responsive room (likely the spine)
Signal's responsiveness (as behaviour) + Things-like clarity, haptics & restraint + real diaspora voices as the cultural layer

A calm, light, grown-up space where the only things that really move are your speech and the ●. Warmth comes from voices and micro-interactions, not decoration. Scores high on nearly every Day-190 quality; resolves Signal's coldness with human voice rather than illustration.

B · The companion that keeps your progress
Finch/Sky faceless attachment (reflected effort, ritual, memory, invest-before-disclose) + capability-based progress as a personal artifact + rare, restrained milestone celebration

Delivers habit, affection and legible progress without streak anxiety — the ● grows subtly from your showing up, and your "record of conversations you can now have" is the progress surface.

C · Living-language moments
Places' cultural richness as a sometimes layer (arrivals, story scenes, milestones) + Memrise-style real people & voices + humour and slang

Gives identity and cultural life their due — the "come into this living language" feeling — without the scaling cost or the everyday density that would compete with learning.

What our product should feel like on Day 190

Opening it feels like stepping into a warm, quiet room where a language you're becoming fluent in is spoken by real people you're starting to understand. The ● wakes to greet you, a little fuller than the day you started because you kept showing up. You speak; it listens and lights up; a voice answers and you follow more of it than last week. Nothing shouts. There's no streak to dread, no leaderboard to lose — just the quiet, true, growing evidence that you can now talk to someone. That's the product: not a course you complete, but a living language you've been let into — and a companion, made of almost nothing, that's been keeping your progress the whole time.
